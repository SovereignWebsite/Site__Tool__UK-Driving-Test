Highway Code

Vulnerable road users and large vehicles

The Highway Code puts the most responsibility on those who can do the most harm. These rules are the detail.

  • Rule H1Everyone is responsible for the safety of others, but those in charge of vehicles that can cause the most harm carry the greatest responsibility to reduce danger.
  • Rule 205Children, especially, may step into the road without warning. Drive at a speed that suits the conditions with that in mind.
  • Rule 207Some pedestrians are particularly vulnerable: children, older people and people with disabilities. Speed matters hugely. At 40 mph a pedestrian struck will probably be killed; at 20 mph the chance is far lower.
  • Rule 208Near schools drive slowly, and watch for young cyclists and pedestrians.
  • Rule 210You must stop when a school crossing patrol shows a ‘Stop for children’ sign.
  • Rule 211Motorcyclists and cyclists are often hard to see, especially at junctions, roundabouts, when overtaking you or filtering through traffic. Look carefully.
  • Rule 212Give motorcyclists, cyclists, horse riders and pedestrians in the road at least as much room as you would a car, and more in bad weather and at night.
  • Rule 213Cyclists may ride in the centre of the lane on narrow roads, at junctions and in slow traffic. It is safer for them, so allow it.
  • Rule 214Passing animals: slow down, give room, and do not scare them with the horn or revving.
  • Rule 215Pass horse riders and horse-drawn vehicles wide and slowly.
  • Rule 217Be patient with learner and newly qualified drivers.
  • Rule 219When an emergency vehicle approaches, stay calm, think about its route, and let it pass while following traffic signs. Avoid stopping before a bend, brow or narrow section.
  • Rule 221Large vehicles need extra road space to turn, and their drivers cannot see directly behind. If you cannot see their mirrors, they cannot see you.
  • Rule 223Give priority to buses and trams when it is safe, especially when they signal to pull away.
