Highway Code

Speed, space and stopping

A limit is a ceiling, not a target. These rules cover how fast, how far back, and how to stop.

  • Rule 124You must not exceed the limit for the road and your vehicle. Roads with street lights generally have a 30 mph limit (20 mph in Wales) unless signs say otherwise. For a car: 60 mph on a single carriageway, 70 mph on a dual carriageway or motorway. Cars towing have lower limits, so check the table.
  • Rule 125The limit is the absolute maximum. It does not make that speed safe in every condition, and unsafe speed makes collisions both more likely and more severe.
  • Rule 126Drive so you can stop well within the distance you can see to be clear. Allow at least a two-second gap on fast roads, at least double that in the wet, and up to ten times as much on ice.
  • Rule 117Normal braking: brake early and lightly, firmer as you begin to stop, easing off just before you stop.
  • Rule 118In an emergency, brake straight away, but try not to lock the wheels, because locked wheels can mean losing control.
  • Rule 119Skids usually come from braking, accelerating or steering too harshly, or from going too fast for the conditions. Remove the cause, for example by easing off.
  • Rule 122Coasting (neutral or clutch down) reduces your control, so avoid it.
  • Rule 146Fit your driving to the road and conditions. Do not treat speed limits as targets.
  • Rule 152In residential streets drive slowly and carefully: people, cyclists and parked cars are likely, and some areas have a 20 mph limit.
  • Rule 153Slow down for traffic calming such as humps and chicanes, and give cyclists and motorcyclists room.
  • Rule 154Country roads: slow for bends, which can be sharper than they look, and for hidden junctions. Expect walkers, riders, farm vehicles and mud.
