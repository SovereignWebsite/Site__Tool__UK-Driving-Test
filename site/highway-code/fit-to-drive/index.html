Highway Code

Fit to drive and paying attention

Before the car matters, the driver does. These rules are about being well enough, awake enough and focused enough to drive.

  • Rule 90You must be fit to drive. You must tell the DVLA about any health condition that is likely to affect your driving.
  • Rule 91Tiredness greatly raises the risk of a collision. Do not start a journey tired, get enough sleep before a long one, and plan to avoid long drives in the small hours when alertness is naturally at its lowest.
  • Rule 92In good daylight you must be able to read a number plate from 20 metres (20.5 metres for the old style of plate). If you need glasses or contact lenses to do that, wear them every time you drive.
  • Rule 93If bright sunlight dazzles you, slow down, and stop if you need to.
  • Rule 95Do not drink and drive. Alcohol seriously affects judgement and ability. The legal limits differ between England and Wales and Scotland, so read the rule for the figures.
  • Rule 96You must not drive under the influence of drugs or medicine. Ask a doctor or pharmacist about any medicine, and do not drive if you are told you may be impaired.
  • Rule 148Safe driving needs concentration. Avoid distractions such as loud music, reading maps, fiddling with the radio, arguing, eating, drinking and smoking.
  • Rule 149You must keep proper control of the vehicle. You must not use a hand-held phone or similar device while driving or supervising a learner, even if you are stopped in traffic. There are narrow exceptions, such as calling 999 in a genuine emergency when it is unsafe or impractical to stop.
  • Rule 150Sat navs and other in-vehicle systems can distract you. You stay responsible for the vehicle, and driver assistance does not change that.
