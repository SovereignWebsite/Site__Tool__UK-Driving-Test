Highway Code

Motorways

Faster traffic and less time to react. These rules keep you safe and keep traffic flowing.

  • Rule 253Certain road users must not use motorways, including pedestrians, cyclists and horse riders. A provisional car licence holder must not drive on one unless accompanied by an approved driving instructor in a dual-control car showing L plates (D plates in Wales).
  • Rule 254Traffic moves faster, so use your mirrors earlier and look much further ahead.
  • Rule 257Flashing amber signals warn of a hazard ahead. Slow down and be ready. Speed up only once you pass a signal that is not flashing or a sign showing the national speed limit or ‘END’.
  • Rule 258A red flashing signal or red ‘X’ means a closed lane. You must not drive in it. If all lanes are closed the road is closed.
  • Rule 259When joining, give priority to traffic already on the motorway, match its speed, and do not cross solid white lines or use the hard shoulder.
  • Rule 261You must not exceed a speed limit shown in a red circle, or the limit for your vehicle.
  • Rule 262Motorway driving can make you sleepy. Take breaks at service areas.
  • Rule 263You must not reverse, cross the central reservation or drive against the flow. If you miss your exit, carry on to the next one.
  • Rule 264Keep in the left lane unless overtaking, and return to it when safe.
  • Rule 267Overtake on the right only, after checking mirrors and blind spots, and take care not to cut in. Look out for motorcyclists.
  • Rule 268Do not overtake on the left. In congested traffic you may keep up with your own lane even if it passes a slower lane to the right, but do not weave.
  • Rule 269You must not use the hard shoulder except in an emergency, or when directed by police, traffic officers or a sign.
  • Rule 271You must not stop on the carriageway, hard shoulder, slip road, central reservation or verge except in an emergency or when told to. Do not stop to make or take phone calls.
  • Rule 274After leaving, your speed may be higher than you realise. 50 mph can feel like 30 mph, so check your speedometer.
