Highway Code

Driving in bad weather

Grip, sight and control all change with the weather. So should how you drive.

  • Rule 226Use headlights when visibility is seriously reduced, generally when you cannot see further than 100 metres. Fog lights only then, and off again when it clears.
  • Rule 227In the wet, stopping distances are at least double. Keep well back, ease off if the steering goes light, and take extra care around vulnerable road users.
  • Rule 228In icy or snowy weather do not drive unless your journey is essential. If it is, take great care and allow more time.
  • Rule 229Clear all snow and ice from every window, the mirrors, lights and number plates before setting off.
  • Rule 230On ice and snow, stopping distances can be ten times greater than on dry roads. Keep well back.
  • Rule 231On ice, drive slowly in as high a gear as possible, and accelerate and brake very gently.
  • Rule 232Strong wind affects high-sided vehicles most, but can also blow cars, cyclists, motorcyclists and horse riders off course, especially on open stretches.
  • Rule 234Before entering fog, check your mirrors and slow down. Fog can suddenly be thicker than it looks.
  • Rule 235In fog keep a safe distance and be able to stop well within what you can see. Rear lights ahead can give a false sense of security.
  • Rule 236Do not use fog lights unless visibility is seriously reduced: they dazzle others and can hide your brake lights.
  • Rule 237In hot weather keep the vehicle ventilated to avoid drowsiness, and be aware the road surface can soften, or become slippery when it rains after a dry spell.
