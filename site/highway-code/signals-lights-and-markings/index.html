Highway Code

Signals, lights, the horn and road markings

How you talk to other road users, and how the road talks to you.

  • Rule 103Signals warn and inform other road users, including pedestrians. Give clear signals in good time, having checked the signal is not misleading.
  • Rule 104Watch for other people’s signals, and remember that an indicator may have been left on by mistake.
  • Rule 105You must obey signals from police officers, traffic officers, traffic wardens and school crossing patrols.
  • Rule 110Flash your headlights only to let others know you are there. Never use them to send any other message.
  • Rule 111Never assume a flash of headlights is an invitation to go. Use your own judgement and proceed carefully.
  • Rule 112Use the horn only while moving and only to warn of your presence. Never sound it aggressively. There are also restrictions on using it while stationary in a built-up area at night.
  • Rule 113Sidelights and rear plate lights must be lit between sunset and sunrise, and you use headlights at night except on roads with street lighting.
  • Rule 114Do not dazzle other road users. Use front or rear fog lights only when visibility is seriously reduced, and switch them off when it improves.
  • Rule 116Hazard warning lights may be used when stationary to warn that you are temporarily obstructing traffic. You must not use them while driving, except on a motorway or unrestricted dual carriageway to warn of a hazard or obstruction ahead.
  • Rule 127A broken white line marks the centre of the road. When the line lengthens and the gaps shorten, there is a hazard ahead.
  • Rule 129Double white lines with the solid line nearest you: you must not cross or straddle it, except in the limited cases the rule lists, such as turning into a side road.
  • Rule 132Reflective studs: white mark lanes or the middle of the road, red mark the left edge, amber mark the central reservation, green mark lay-by and slip road edges.
