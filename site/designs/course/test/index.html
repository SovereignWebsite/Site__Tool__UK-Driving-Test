Sources and licence

How the theory test works

The theory test is the first of the two tests you pass before you hold a full car licence. It has two parts, taken as one test. Everything on this page is taken from GOV.UK’s pages on the car theory test, read on 24 November 2026. GOV.UK is the authority: check it before you book.

Two parts, one test

You must have a provisional driving licence to book. The test has multiple-choice questions and a hazard perception test (a video test about spotting hazards on the road). You book and take them as a single test, and you must pass both parts to pass.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: booking your test, read on 24 November 2026.

When you can take it

You can take the theory test from your 17th birthday. You can take it from your 16th birthday if you get, or have applied for, the higher weekly rate of the mobility part of Personal Independence Payment (PIP).

You usually need to pass it before you can get your full car driving licence. GOV.UK lists a few people who do not need the car theory test, for example someone upgrading an automatic car licence to a manual one.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: booking your test, read on 24 November 2026.

Part one: multiple-choice questions

  • You have 57 minutes to answer 50 multiple-choice questions.
  • A question and several possible answers appear on screen, and you choose the right one.
  • Three of the questions are about a short, silent video of a normal driving situation. You can watch it as many times as you like.
  • You can flag questions to come back to, and you can go back and change any answer at any point.
  • You do not have to use the full 57 minutes. You can have a break of up to 3 minutes before the hazard perception part starts.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: multiple-choice questions, read on 24 November 2026.

Part two: hazard perception

You watch 14 video clips of everyday road scenes. Each has at least one developing hazard, and one clip has two. You score up to 5 points for each developing hazard, so the part is worth up to 75 points. You only get one attempt at each clip. Read more on the hazard perception page.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: hazard perception test, read on 24 November 2026.

You get the result at the test centre. If you pass, you get a letter with a pass certificate number, which you need to book and take your driving test. The number lasts for 2 years, and you must pass your driving test in that time or pass the theory test again.

If you fail, the letter tells you which parts you did not score enough on. You must take the full test again, even if you passed one part, and wait at least 3 working days.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: pass mark and test result, read on 24 November 2026.

On the day

  • Take your UK photocard driving licence. If you have a paper licence, bring a valid passport as well. Your test is cancelled, and you do not get your money back, if you do not take the right things.
  • Arrive 15 minutes before your test starts. If you are late, the test is cancelled and you lose your money.
  • Turn off all electronic devices before you enter, including phones, smart watches and fitness trackers. You store personal belongings in a locker, or a clear plastic box if there are no lockers.
  • You pass identity and security checks first. Staff need to see your face to check it against your photo ID.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: what to take, read on 24 November 2026.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: when you arrive, read on 24 November 2026.

If you need support

When you book, say if you have a reading difficulty, disability or health condition. GOV.UK describes options such as hearing the test through headphones, extra time, and taking the test in British Sign Language. Read the full details on GOV.UK, because some options need proof or notice.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: reading difficulty, disability or health condition, read on 24 November 2026.

Booking, fees and official practice

This site does not quote the test fee or booking rules, because they can change. Use GOV.UK: Book your theory test. GOV.UK also offers a free practice theory test and lists official books and apps in its revision and practice page.

GOV.UK says the multiple-choice questions are based on three books: The Highway Code, Know your traffic signs, and Driving: the essential skills. This site covers The Highway Code only, for now.

Source: GOV.UK, Theory test: revision and practice, read on 24 November 2026.
