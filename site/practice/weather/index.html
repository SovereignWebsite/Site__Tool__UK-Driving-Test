Quiz: Driving in bad weather

8 practice questions written by this site, not official DVSA questions. Choose one answer for each, then check. Each explanation names the Highway Code rule it comes from. Your result is saved only in this browser, if you allow it.

  1. Question 1 of 8 In wet weather, how do stopping distances compare with dry roads?

    The answer is: At least double

    In wet weather stopping distances will be at least double those required on dry roads.

    Highway Code: Rule 227

  2. Question 2 of 8 When must you use headlights in daytime because of poor visibility?

    The answer is: When you cannot see for more than 100 metres

    You must use headlights when visibility is seriously reduced, generally when you cannot see more than 100 metres.

    Highway Code: Rule 226

  3. Question 3 of 8 Before driving in snow, what must you do?

    The answer is: Clear all snow and ice from all windows, mirrors, lights and plates

    You must be able to see, and lights and plates must be clean and legible, so clear snow and ice from all of them.

    Highway Code: Rule 229

  4. Question 4 of 8 On an icy road, how much greater can stopping distances be than on a dry road?

    The answer is: Up to ten times

    Stopping distances on ice and snow can be ten times greater.

    Highway Code: Rule 230, Rule 126

  5. Question 5 of 8 What gear should you use on an icy road?

    The answer is: As high a gear as possible

    Drive slowly in as high a gear as possible and accelerate and brake very gently.

    Highway Code: Rule 231

  6. Question 6 of 8 You enter fog and it seems to be clearing. What should you assume?

    The answer is: You may suddenly meet thicker fog, so stay careful

    Even if fog seems to be clearing you can suddenly find yourself in thick fog.

    Highway Code: Rule 234

  7. Question 7 of 8 Why should you keep a distance from a high-sided vehicle in strong wind?

    The answer is: Gusts can affect cyclists, riders and cars as well, and turbulence can affect motorcyclists

    High-sided vehicles are most affected by wind, but gusts can blow cars, cyclists and riders off course, and turbulence from large vehicles affects motorcyclists.

    Highway Code: Rule 232, Rule 233

  8. Question 8 of 8 Your steering feels light in heavy rain. What does this probably mean, and what should you do?

    The answer is: Water may be stopping the tyres gripping; ease off and slow down gradually

    If steering becomes unresponsive, water is probably stopping the tyres gripping the road. Ease off the accelerator and slow down gradually.

    Highway Code: Rule 227
