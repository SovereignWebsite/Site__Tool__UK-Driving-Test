Quiz: Speed, space and stopping

8 practice questions written by this site, not official DVSA questions. Choose one answer for each, then check. Each explanation names the Highway Code rule it comes from. Your result is saved only in this browser, if you allow it.

  1. Question 1 of 8 What is the national speed limit for a car on a single carriageway?

    The answer is: 60 mph

    For a car, the Code’s table gives 60 mph on single carriageways and 70 mph on dual carriageways and motorways.

    Highway Code: Rule 124

  2. Question 2 of 8 A road has street lights and no speed limit signs. What limit generally applies in England?

    The answer is: 30 mph

    Roads with street lights generally have a 30 mph limit unless signs show otherwise. In Wales the generally applicable limit is 20 mph.

    Highway Code: Rule 124

  3. Question 3 of 8 The speed limit is 60 mph but the road is icy. What does the limit mean?

    The answer is: It is the absolute maximum, not a target

    The limit is the absolute maximum and does not mean it is safe to drive at that speed in all conditions.

    Highway Code: Rule 125, Rule 146

  4. Question 4 of 8 On a fast dry road, what is the minimum gap to leave from the vehicle in front?

    The answer is: Two seconds

    Allow at least a two-second gap on high-speed roads, doubled in the wet.

    Highway Code: Rule 126

  5. Question 5 of 8 It is raining. How should you change your gap to the vehicle in front?

    The answer is: At least double it

    In the wet the gap should be at least doubled, because tyres have less grip.

    Highway Code: Rule 126, Rule 227

  6. Question 6 of 8 What is the safest way to brake in normal driving?

    The answer is: Brake early and lightly, firmer as you slow, easing off before you stop

    The safest way is to brake early and lightly, then more firmly, then ease off just before stopping.

    Highway Code: Rule 117

  7. Question 7 of 8 Your car starts to skid. What is the usual first step?

    The answer is: Remove the cause, for example ease off the brake or accelerator

    Skids are usually caused by harsh braking, accelerating or steering, or excess speed. Remove the cause.

    Highway Code: Rule 119

  8. Question 8 of 8 Why should you avoid coasting in neutral or with the clutch down?

    The answer is: It reduces your control

    Coasting removes engine braking and can reduce control, including steering response and brake effectiveness.

    Highway Code: Rule 122
