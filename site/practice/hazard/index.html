Quiz: Hazard awareness

6 practice questions written by this site, not official DVSA questions. Choose one answer for each, then check. Each explanation names the Highway Code rule it comes from. Your result is saved only in this browser, if you allow it.

  1. Question 1 of 6 A parked car at the side of the road has nobody in it and shows no signs of moving. Is it a developing hazard?

    The answer is: Not yet: it is only a potential hazard until something about it changes

    GOV.UK describes a developing hazard as something that would cause you to take action, such as changing speed or direction. A static parked car is not doing that yet, so it is only a potential hazard. Rule 206 says to take care when passing parked vehicles, so keep watching it.

    Highway Code: Rule 206. Also GOV.UK, Theory test: hazard perception test, read on 24 November 2026

  2. Question 2 of 6 You are passing parked cars on a residential street and a ball rolls out between two of them. What is the best response?

    The answer is: Slow down, because a child may follow it

    The ball is this site’s own scenario. The rules’ point is that pedestrians, especially children, may step unexpectedly into the road, and may emerge from between parked vehicles. So slow down and be ready to stop.

    Highway Code: Rule 205, Rule 206

  3. Question 3 of 6 You are approaching a junction and a cyclist ahead looks over their shoulder. What does it suggest?

    The answer is: They may be about to pull out or change direction, so give them time and room

    If a rider looks over their shoulder it could mean they intend to pull out, turn right or change direction.

    Highway Code: Rule 212

  4. Question 4 of 6 A bus ahead is at a stop with its right indicator on. What should you expect?

    The answer is: It may pull away, so give it priority if safe

    Give priority to buses when it is safe, especially when they signal to pull away from stops. Look out for people getting off.

    Highway Code: Rule 223

  5. Question 5 of 6 On a country lane you approach a sharp bend with a hedge on the inside. What is the hazard?

    The answer is: The bend may be sharper than it looks and something may be round it

    Bends can be sharper than they appear, and you may meet horse riders, cyclists, slow vehicles or mud.

    Highway Code: Rule 154

  6. Question 6 of 6 This site’s own scenario: you see a lorry ahead with its left indicator on, but it moves out to the right first. What is the lesson?

    The answer is: Large vehicles may need extra room to turn, so stay back and do not assume

    Rule 221 says large vehicles may need extra road space to turn, and rule 104 says an indicator on another vehicle may not have been cancelled. So stay back and watch what the vehicle actually does.

    Highway Code: Rule 221, Rule 104
